# Style guidance

## The type scale

Three sizes: title, body, caption. A fourth is a decision the audience will make
wrongly.

| Role | Size | Weight | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cover title | 40–48 pt | Bold | Ink |
| Slide title | 28–36 pt | Bold or semibold | Ink |
| Body | 18–22 pt | Regular | Ink |
| Lead-in number | Same as body + bold | Bold | Accent |
| Caption, source | 12–14 pt | Regular | Muted |

44 pt belongs to covers; content titles stay 28–36 pt.

- One typeface family. A second family means "different voice": use it for one
  thing (a quote, a code line) and nothing else.
- Bold is emphasis, not decoration: at most one bold phrase per paragraph.
- All-caps suits a 2–4 word label; in a sentence it is a readability tax.
- Left-align body text. Centred paragraphs over two lines are hard to track back.

## Colour

- One ink, one muted, one accent, plus the template's rule colour. More and the
  accent stops meaning "look here".
- Contrast is a correctness issue: body on white needs dark ink (≈4.5:1), and
  text over a photograph needs a scrim, not luck.
- Never encode meaning in colour alone: an accent bar also needs an arrow or a
  word.
- Chart series colours come from the theme; a sixth series colour signals too
  many series.

## Density

- Body blocks stop at about 6 lines of 18 pt on a 16:9 slide.
- A slide table reads at about 7 rows × 4 columns. Beyond that it is an appendix,
  and the slide carries the one row that matters.
- Keep at least 0.6 in between content blocks and the slide edge.
- One idea per slide. If the title needs "and", split.

## Cover art direction

A cover carries three things: the title, the context line, and the visual that
sets the tone. Keep the roles separate.

- Abstract geometry, a photograph of the actual subject, or a flat colour field
  all work. Clip art of a handshake does not.
- Photographic visual: scrim it at 40–60% black under the text rather than
  hoping the text lands on a dark region.
- No suitable image: colour field plus an accent rule. An invented diagram
  pretending to be a photograph is worse than a plain cover.
- Encode the deck's palette: an amber accent means an amber-bearing visual, not a
  random sunset.
- Never ship a screenshot of a tool, a UI or another slide as cover art.

## Closing slides

The last slide restates the ask in one line and nothing else. "Thank you" alone
wastes the slot the audience looks at while gathering their things.
