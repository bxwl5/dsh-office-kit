# Writing quality

Content quality is the part of a document the format cannot fix. These are the rules to apply while drafting, before any rendering.

## Structure before prose

- Every heading states a claim the reader can disagree with ("Services carried the quarter", not "Services"). A heading that only names a topic is a missed opportunity to be clear.
- A section that cannot be summarised in one sentence is two sections.
- Three levels of heading is the practical maximum; a fourth usually means the content should be a table or an appendix.
- Nothing between a heading and its first paragraph. No label line, no restated title.

## Paragraphs

- One idea per paragraph, first sentence carries it.
- 3–6 sentences is the comfortable range; a 1600-word paragraph is a formatting defect even if the prose is good.
- No em-dash pile-ups, no "furthermore/moreover" chains, no sentence that only announces the next sentence.
- The last paragraph of a section says what it meant, not that it is finished.

## Lists and tables

- Bullets for parallel items of equal weight; never for a sequence (that is a numbered list).
- If items have two attributes, a table beats a bulleted list with parentheticals.
- Table headers are nouns, units in the header ("Revenue ($m)"), numbers right-aligned.
- Every table earns its caption; a table without a purpose is a screenshot of data.

## Voice

- Active unless the actor is unknown or unimportant; "We found", not "It was found".
- Present tense for findings, past for actions taken.
- No unsupported superlatives. "18% growth" beats "strong growth"; if a number is unknown, say so rather than adding an adverb.
- Define an acronym once, then use it. Never define it twice in different places.

## Numbers

- Consistent precision. Do not mix "12.4%" and "8%" in one table unless the source did.
- Percentages below 1 take a leading zero (0.4%, not .4%).
- State the period and the currency in the table or its caption, not in the surrounding prose.
- A number in prose must match the number in the table; rendering does not catch this, re-reading does.

## What to cut

- Opening paragraphs that explain that the document will explain something.
- Closing paragraphs that thank the reader.
- Bold on every second phrase: emphasis used everywhere is emphasis used nowhere.
- Sentences that begin "It is important to note that".
