# Writing quality

Content quality is the part of a document the format cannot fix. Apply these rules while drafting, before any rendering.

## Structure before prose

- Every heading states a claim the reader can disagree with ("Services carried the quarter", not "Services").
- A section that cannot be summarised in one sentence is two sections.
- Three heading levels is the practical maximum; a fourth means the content wants a table or an appendix.
- Nothing between a heading and its first paragraph — no label line, no restated title.

## Paragraphs

- One idea per paragraph, carried by the first sentence.
- 3–6 sentences is comfortable; a 1600-word paragraph is a formatting defect even when the prose is good.
- No em-dash pile-ups, no "furthermore/moreover" chains, no sentence that only announces the next one.
- The last paragraph of a section says what the section meant, not that it is finished.

## Lists and tables

- Bullets for parallel items of equal weight; never for a sequence (that is a numbered list).
- Items with two attributes belong in a table, not a bulleted list with parentheticals.
- Table headers are nouns with units ("Revenue ($m)"); numbers right-aligned.
- Every table earns its caption; a table without a purpose is a screenshot of data.

## Voice

- Active unless the actor is unknown or unimportant: "We found", not "It was found".
- Present tense for findings, past for actions taken.
- No unsupported superlatives: "18% growth" beats "strong growth"; if the number is unknown, say so instead of adding an adverb.
- Define an acronym once, then use it; never redefine it elsewhere.

## Numbers

- Consistent precision: do not mix "12.4%" and "8%" in one table unless the source did.
- Percentages below 1 take a leading zero (0.4%, not .4%).
- State period and currency in the table or its caption, not in surrounding prose.
- A prose number must match the table number; rendering does not catch this, re-reading does.

## What to cut

- Opening paragraphs explaining that the document will explain something.
- Closing paragraphs thanking the reader.
- Bold on every second phrase: emphasis used everywhere is emphasis used nowhere.
- Sentences beginning "It is important to note that".
